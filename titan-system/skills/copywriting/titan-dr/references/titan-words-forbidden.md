# TITAN COMPLIANCE & FORBIDDEN WORDS

## THE TWO-TIER RISK SYSTEM

Two separate risk systems govern supplement advertising. Confuse them and you either lose your account or leave money on the table.

- **FDA RISK** = Regulatory grey area. Could trigger warning letter years from now. But ads run TODAY.
- **META BAN RISK** = Automated bot flags. Ad rejected in minutes. Account disabled. No traffic TODAY.

**Strategy: Avoid Tier 1 (instant bans). Embrace Tier 2 (FDA grey area) with Shields. This is where the money is made.**

## TIER 1 — NEVER USE (FDA Disease Claims)

These words, when connected to a product in a way that implies treatment, diagnosis, cure, or prevention of disease, constitute an illegal disease claim. Zero tolerance. No exceptions.

Meta's automated systems flag these in minutes. Repeated violations = account disabled.

| Trigger | Why It Kills |
|---------|-------------|
| "Weight Loss" (exact phrase) | Flagged by Meta health AI |
| Before/After body transformation photos | Explicitly banned in Meta policy |
| "YOU + Negative Attribute" | Personal Attributes Policy violation |
| "Cures" / "Treats" / "Prevents" | Medical claim — instant flag |
| Drug name comparisons | Pharmaceutical category trigger |
| Cancer, HIV, Alzheimer's, COVID claims | Serious disease category |
| Fake play buttons / UI elements | Deceptive UI violation |
| "Guaranteed results" / "Instant relief" | Unsubstantiated claim trigger |

**Tier 1 Extended — FDA Death Zone:**
- cure / cures / cured
- treat / treats / treatment / treating
- diagnose / diagnosis
- prevent / prevents / prevention (in disease context)
- disease / disorder / syndrome (in benefit claims)
- heal / heals / healing (body-level — organ or disease healing)
- repair (in disease/injury context — e.g., "repairs liver damage")
- eliminate (in disease context)
- reverse (in disease context — "reverses diabetes" = illegal)
- "fights cancer" / "prevents cancer" / "kills cancer cells"
- "reverses diabetes" / "controls blood sugar" (disease framing)
- "prevents heart disease" / "treats cardiovascular disease"
- "kills bacteria" / "eliminates pathogens" (drug claim)
- "treats depression" / "cures anxiety" / "treats ADHD"

> **Rule:** If the sentence could appear on a prescription drug label, it's a Tier 1 violation.

## THE 6 SHIELD PROTOCOLS

Required whenever using any Tier 2 term. These make the difference between legal and illegal copy.

**SHIELD 1: The Asterisk Shield**
Place asterisk (*) on health claims. Link or refer to footer disclaimer.
"Supports healthy gut lining*" — asterisk links to FDA disclaimer.

**SHIELD 2: The FDA Disclaimer (Use This Exact Copy)**
> *"The statements made on this website have not been evaluated by the Food & Drug Administration. These products are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

**SHIELD 3: The Testimonial Disclaimer**
> "Results are unverified and may not reflect the typical purchaser's experience, may not apply to the average person, and are not intended to represent or guarantee that anyone will achieve the same or similar results."

**SHIELD 4: The Testimonial Loophole**
Put aggressive claims INSIDE testimonials attributed to real, named customers. This shifts liability from brand claim to user story.
- Example: "My wife and I faced infertility challenges, and adding this helped us heal. — Tuf Cooper, 4x World Champion"
- Deploy: Named customer + specific condition + personal experience language

**SHIELD 5: The Study Citation Shield**
Disease names appear in context of citing external research — NOT direct product claims.
- "Harvard University researchers found: 93% improved or resolved obesity..."
- Frame: "The study says..." not "Our product does..."

**SHIELD 6: The Founder Story Defense**
Aggressive condition names appear as PROBLEMS in personal narrative, not product benefit claims.
- "Years ago I struggled with gut issues which led to: Weight Gain, Inflammation, Low Energy."
- These are personal history, not product promises.

## THE PERSONAL ATTRIBUTES TRAP (Most Common Meta Rejection)

- BANNED: "Are you struggling with anxiety?" / "Are you overweight?" / "Do you have brain fog?"
- APPROVED: "Many people over 40 notice their energy isn't what it used to be..."
- **Rule:** Shift from second-person accusation to third-person observation. Never assert the viewer HAS a condition. Describe it as something that HAPPENS to people.

## ADS vs. ON-PAGE COPY — SCOPE RULES

**Meta Ads:** Bot-scanned in real time. Tier 1 = instant rejection. Tier 2 = runs with shields. Strict zone.

**On-Page Copy (landing pages, listicles, advertorials):** NOT scanned the same way. More freedom. FDA/FTC still applies, but Meta's automated enforcement is focused on the ad unit.

**Our approach for on-page — conservative:**
Even with more freedom, always maintain:
- FDA footer disclaimer (Shield 2)
- Asterisks on health claims (Shield 1)
- Aggressive claims inside named testimonials (Shield 4)
- Disease/condition stats cited from external research (Shield 5)
- Testimonial disclaimer present (Shield 3)

**Why not go aggressive like competitors?** FDA can issue warning letters for on-page claims regardless of Meta's enforcement. The shields are invisible to readers and cost nothing.

## META / FACEBOOK AD POLICY VIOLATIONS

Meta's ad review system flags specific language patterns. These will get ads rejected or accounts restricted.

### Before/After Prohibition
Meta bans images and copy that imply dramatic transformation:
- ✗ "Before: struggling / After: thriving"
- ✗ Placing side-by-side comparison images implying dramatic physical changes
- ✗ "Lost 47 pounds" as a standalone claim (acceptable in testimonials with appropriate disclaimers)

### Personal Attribute Targeting Language
Meta prohibits copy that implies awareness of the user's personal health status:
- ✗ "You have leaky gut" (implies knowledge of their condition)
- ✗ "Your gut is damaged" (same)
- ✗ "You're struggling with bloating" (too personal, too specific)
- ✓ "Many people struggle with bloating" (general framing — compliant)
- ✓ "If you've been dealing with gut issues..." (conditional — compliant)

### "YOU + Negative" Framing — NEVER USE
Directly telling a user something is wrong with THEM:
- ✗ "You are overweight"
- ✗ "Your gut is unhealthy"
- ✗ "You have inflammation"
- ✓ "People with gut imbalance often find..." (third person)
- ✓ "If you sometimes feel bloated..." (conditional, non-accusatory)

### Banned Advertising Categories
These topics require special ad account permissions or are fully prohibited:
- Prescription drug promotion
- Unsubstantiated weight loss claims
- Multi-level marketing (with income claims)
- Gambling
- Tobacco / vaping

### Urgency Language Restrictions
Countdown timers and urgency claims that are fake or misleading get flagged:
- ✗ "Offer expires in 3 hours" (evergreen — never actually expires)
- ✓ "Limited batch — only 847 units remain from this harvest" (real constraint, real reason)
- ✓ "Price increases [specific date]" (if true)

## THE TITAN ZONE — 5 COPY TECHNIQUES

The "Titan Zone" is the precise space where claims are strong enough to convert but structured to survive automated review AND regulatory scrutiny.

**The Bot vs. Human Paradox:** You are always writing for two audiences simultaneously:
- **The Bot (Meta AI):** Scans for keywords, phrases, image patterns. Cannot understand implied meaning. Flags patterns, not intent.
- **The Human (Consumer):** Responds to context, emotion, implied meaning, transformation stories.

**Core Principle:** Write VANILLA for the Bot (avoid trigger patterns) while writing SPICY for the Human (convey benefits through subtext, story, and implication).

---

### Technique 1: The User Story Defense

First-person testimonial language shifts claims from objective facts to subjective experiences. Subjective experiences cannot be proven false — they are personal accounts.

| ❌ Objective Claim (Risky) | ✅ Subjective Experience (Safer) |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------|
| This supplement supports fat burning | "I felt like the weight was finally shifting" |
| Eliminates joint discomfort | "My knees felt 20 years younger" |
| Cures poor sleep | "For the first time in years, I slept through the night" |
| Boosts energy levels | "I felt like I had endless energy" |
| Improves memory | "I felt sharper than I had in decades" |

---

### Technique 2: The Mechanism Focus

Instead of claiming specific outcomes, explain HOW the product works at a biological level. This shifts attention from guaranteed results to scientific plausibility — which is both more credible and more compliant.

| ❌ Outcome Claim (Risky) | ✅ Mechanism Focus (Safer) |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Burns belly fat | Supports your body's natural metabolic function |
| Cures digestive problems | Supports the growth of beneficial gut bacteria |
| Eliminates joint pain | Helps maintain healthy cartilage tissue |
| Boosts immunity | Provides nutritional support for immune cell function |

**The Ingredient Story:** Focus on what the ingredient does at a biological level:
- "First-day colostrum contains naturally occurring growth factors that support gut lining health*"
- "Beef liver is nature's multivitamin, rich in bioavailable nutrients"

---

### Technique 3: The "What If" Frame

Rhetorical questions plant ideas without making statements. The question format implies possibility without asserting fact — legally and mechanically different from a direct claim.

| ❌ Statement (Assertive) | ✅ Question (Implied) |
|-------------------------|----------------------|
| This will help you feel better | What if you could finally feel like yourself again? |
| You'll have more energy | What would you do with boundless energy every day? |
| Your discomfort will disappear | Imagine waking up without that morning stiffness... |
| You'll sleep better | What if tonight was the first night of truly restful sleep? |

**Imagination triggers:** Words like "imagine," "picture," and "what if" activate the reader's own imagination. They make the claim — you don't.

---

### Technique 4: The Qualifier Arsenal

Qualifying language converts absolute claims into defensible statements. Stack qualifiers for maximum protection.

| ❌ Absolute (Risky) | ✅ Qualified (Safer) |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| Eliminates | May help reduce |
| Guarantees | Designed to support |
| Proven to | Studies suggest |
| Will give you | May contribute to |
| Always works | Many users report |

**Qualifier stacking:** "Studies suggest this ingredient may help support healthy gut function in some individuals.*" — three qualifiers in one sentence.

---

### Technique 5: Puffery vs. Fact

Puffery is exaggerated, subjective claims no reasonable consumer would take literally. It is legally protected as opinion — not a factual assertion requiring substantiation.

| ✅ Puffery (Legal Opinion) | ❌ Factual Claim (Must Substantiate) |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| The best colostrum on the market | Clinically proven to reduce inflammation |
| You'll feel amazing | Reduces discomfort by 40% |
| Life-changing formula | Helps you lose 20 pounds in 30 days |
| Revolutionary breakthrough | 95% success rate |

**Rule:** If a reasonable consumer would interpret it as an objective, measurable claim — it's a fact that needs substantiation. If they'd read it as enthusiastic opinion — it's puffery.
